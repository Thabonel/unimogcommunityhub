## Q21: What are the steps to remove the cab for major repairs?

**Section**: Section 60.5: Raising and Lowering Cab (pages 1071-1098)
**Pages Found**: Pages 1075, 1090, 1095

**Barry's Answer:**

Cab removal is a major procedure requiring proper equipment and sequence:

1. **Cab Tilting Equipment**:
   - Two methods available: With tilting device or without
   - Workshop version with automatic tilting equipment
   - SA 35 990 version with manual controls
   - Manual method without powered equipment

2. **Preparation Steps**:
   - Lower driver's cab into normal driving position
   - Disconnect all electrical connections to cab
   - Disconnect hydraulic lines if equipped with tilting device
   - Disconnect fuel lines from engine to cab
   - Disconnect brake lines if cab-mounted

3. **Tilting Device Removal** (When Cab is Raised):
   - Move control lever to "lower" position
   - Operate hydraulic pump until bearing pin can be withdrawn
   - Operate pump until thin piston rod emerges (third stage)
   - Detach both hydraulic hoses from cylinder
   - Attach dust caps to prevent contamination
   - Release piston rod completely

4. **Cab Raising Procedure**:
   - Mount hydraulic tilting equipment if available
   - Slowly raise cab to approximately 60-degree angle
   - Install safety supports at multiple points
   - Do NOT rely on hydraulic system alone
   - Ensure adequate clearance for work underneath

5. **Cab Lowering Procedure**:
   - Move control lever to "lower" position (if equipped)
   - Operate hydraulic pump slowly and evenly
   - Ensure cab lowers straight without binding
   - Watch for proper seating of mounting points
   - Reconnect all systems in reverse order

6. **Related Procedures**:
   - Renewing roof hatch (page 1095)
   - Renewing tailgate (page 1098)
   - Renewing side doors (separate adjustment)
   - Renewing entrance steps
   - Cable renewal at side entrance

7. **Major Repair Access After Cab Removal**:
   - Complete engine access with cab removed
   - Transmission service simplified
   - Fuel system access improved
   - Cooling system complete access
   - Electrical system complete access

8. **Safety Precautions**:
   - Use proper jacking equipment
   - Install multiple safety supports
   - Never work under cab supported only by hydraulics
   - Keep bystanders clear during operation
   - Ensure adequate workspace below cab

**Complete Reference**: Section 60.5 (Raising and Lowering Cab) pages 1071-1098 contains detailed procedures for all three methods and safety requirements.

## Q22: How is the differential lock system tested and maintained?

**Section**: Section 33: Front Axle (pages 381-410)
**Pages Found**: Pages 24, 523, 537

**Barry's Answer:**

The differential lock system is critical for off-road traction and requires specific maintenance:

1. **Differential Lock Components**:
   - Differential lock actuator assembly
   - Lock engagement/disengagement mechanism
   - Pneumatic or hydraulic activation system
   - Locking differential housing

2. **System Location**:
   - Located in front axle differential housing
   - Engages front wheel drive locks together
   - Controlled from driver's cab
   - Visual or gauged indication of lock status

3. **Testing Procedure**:
   - Engage differential lock switch in cab
   - Listen for audible engagement signal
   - Verify both front wheels respond identically
   - Test turning radius with lock engaged
   - Compare with unlocked turning

4. **Maintenance Requirements**:
   - Regular lock engagement/disengagement cycling
   - Check for smooth operation
   - Listen for abnormal grinding sounds
   - Monitor air/hydraulic pressure
   - Check for fluid leaks

5. **Servicing the Differential Lock**:
   - Clean all connection points
   - Verify proper line routing
   - Check pressure specifications
   - Test relief valve function
   - Lubricate pivot points

6. **Brake Line Integration**:
   - Differential lock line separate from brake lines
   - Brake circuit lines attached to axle housing
   - Proper identification of lines critical
   - Color coding or labeling essential
   - Disconnection procedure requires care

7. **Oil and Lubricant Specifications**:
   - Differential oil capacity specified
   - Fill capacity: Refer to Section 33 data sheet
   - Use recommended gear oil grade
   - Check oil level regularly
   - Oil condition affects lock operation

8. **Operational Data**:
   - Lock torque specifications
   - Preload settings for bearings
   - End play measurements
   - Drive gear specifications

**Differential Lock Testing**: On firm ground, engage the lock and attempt to make a tight turn. If properly functioning, the vehicle should demonstrate extremely tight turning radius with both front wheels locked together. Unlock and verify normal turning returns.

## Q25: How do you inspect and replace the fuel injection pump seals?

**Section**: Engine Fuel System (pages 17-82 & 85-118)
**Pages Found**: Various engine and fuel system sections

**Barry's Answer:**

Fuel injection pump seal service is specialized work requiring precision and expertise:

1. **Fuel Injection Pump Location**:
   - Mounted on engine block
   - Driven by engine camshaft
   - Pressurizes fuel for injectors
   - Critical for engine performance

2. **Seal System Overview**:
   - Multiple seals prevent fuel leakage
   - Pump housing seal at engine connection
   - Drive shaft seal at camshaft interface
   - Fuel outlet port seals
   - Internal piston seals

3. **External Inspection Signs**:
   - Fuel leakage around pump housing
   - Wet or oily appearance on pump exterior
   - Fuel smell around engine
   - Visible wet spots on mounting surface
   - Fuel dripping during operation

4. **Service Procedure Overview**:
   - Disconnect fuel inlet and outlet lines
   - Remove pump mounting bolts
   - Carefully withdraw pump from engine
   - Inspect seal condition
   - Clean all fuel residue

5. **Seal Inspection**:
   - Examine all seals for hardening
   - Check for cracks or damage
   - Look for swelling or shrinkage
   - Verify seal compression
   - Test seal flexibility

6. **Replacement Requirements**:
   - Always replace all seals during service
   - Use OEM seals only
   - Install fresh seals even if old ones appear serviceable
   - Never reuse old seals
   - Follow exact installation sequence

7. **Installation Procedure**:
   - Install new seals in correct sequence
   - Lubricate seals lightly with diesel fuel
   - Position pump carefully on engine
   - Install mounting bolts gradually
   - Tighten to specification in cross pattern

8. **Testing After Service**:
   - Start engine and check for leaks
   - Allow engine to warm up
   - Observe pump for any fuel weeping
   - Run at various RPMs
   - Inspect pump mount after 15-minute run

**Important**: Fuel injection pump seal service on the Mercedes-Benz OM366A is complex and requires specialized knowledge. Improper service can lead to:
- Fuel leakage and fire hazard
- Loss of injection pressure
- Engine damage from contaminated fuel
- Performance degradation

**Recommendation**: This service should be performed by a qualified Mercedes-Benz diesel specialist or authorized service center with proper equipment and expertise.

## Q27: How do you charge the electrical system battery?

**Section**: Electrical System Technical Data (pages 993-1022)
**Pages Found**: Pages 149, 152, 993

**Barry's Answer:**

Electrical system battery charging requires understanding the battery specifications:

1. **Battery Specifications**:

   **12-Volt System**:
   - Voltage: 12 Volts
   - Capacity: 120 Ah (Ampere-hours)
   - Charging current: 5-10% of battery capacity (6-12 Amps)
   - Fast charge to 75%: Maximum 10% charging rate

   **24-Volt System (Two 12V Batteries)**:
   - Voltage: 24 Volts total
   - Capacity options: 2 × 55 Ah or 2 × 125 Ah
   - Charging current: 5-10% of battery capacity
   - Low-temperature test current: 255-625 Amps

2. **Charging Procedure**:
   - Disconnect battery from vehicle
   - Inspect battery condition before charging
   - Clean terminal corrosion if present
   - Connect charger positive to positive terminal
   - Connect charger negative to negative terminal
   - Set charger to appropriate voltage (12V or 24V)
   - Begin charging at specified current rate

3. **Charging Rate Selection**:
   - Normal charging: 5% of capacity per hour
   - Example: 120 Ah battery = 6 Amp charging rate
   - Fast charging: Up to 10% of capacity
   - Example: 120 Ah battery = up to 12 Amps
   - Do not exceed recommended rate

4. **Electrolyte Level**:
   - Check initial electrolyte level
   - Level above upper edge of plates: 10-12mm
   - Maximum level: 5% above upper edge
   - Top up with distilled water if needed before charging

5. **Safety During Charging**:
   - Ensure adequate ventilation
   - Remove metal objects from charging area
   - Never smoke near charging battery
   - Wear safety glasses
   - Keep flammable materials away

6. **Charging Time Estimate**:
   - Full charge time = Battery capacity ÷ Charging rate
   - Example: 120 Ah battery ÷ 6 A charger = 20 hours
   - Fast charge at 10 A = 12 hours to 75% charge
   - Complete charge may take additional time

7. **System Integration**:
   - Alternator charging during engine operation
   - Battery relay isolation when engine off
   - Charging system regulation at specified voltage
   - Over-charge protection built-in

8. **Post-Charging Inspection**:
   - Check terminal cleanliness
   - Verify battery voltage with multimeter
   - Test battery under load if possible
   - Listen for abnormal sounds
   - Smell for unusual odors (sulfur indicates problems)

**Battery Information Table Reference**: Page 993 contains complete battery specifications for all Unimog 435 variants and charging requirements.

## Q29: How do you calibrate the speedometer after tire changes?

**Section**: Main Transmission (pages 207-270)
**Pages Found**: Pages 250, 255, 257

**Barry's Answer:**

Speedometer calibration is integrated with the transmission speedometer drive:

1. **Speedometer Drive System**:
   - Located in transfer case assembly
   - Generates signal proportional to vehicle speed
   - Tire diameter directly affects speed reading
   - Larger tires = lower speed indication
   - Smaller tires = higher speed indication

2. **Transfer Case Speedometer Drive**:
   - Driven by transmission output shaft
   - Intermediate housing assembly contains drive gear
   - Speedometer drive must face upward in assembly
   - Connected to speedometer cable or electrical sender

3. **Effect of Tire Change**:
   - Original tire diameter: Factory specification
   - New tire diameter: Must match original closely
   - Diameter deviation causes speed calibration error
   - Electronic speedometer may require reset

4. **Speedometer Cable Connection** (Transmission Removal/Installation):
   - Disconnect speedometer shaft during transmission removal
   - Remove shift linkage, speedometer shaft, shift indicator
   - Speedometer shaft has seating sleeve for alignment
   - During reinstallation: Attach speedometer shaft with proper seating sleeve
   - Ensure tight connection to prevent noise

5. **Transmission Components Affecting Speed**:
   - Main transmission ratios specified
   - Transfer case ratio
   - Final drive ratio in axles
   - Combined effect on speedometer calibration

6. **Speed Reading Accuracy**:
   - Original equipment tires provide baseline
   - Tire size changes alter actual vehicle speed vs. indicated speed
   - Speedometer cannot be electronically adjusted (mechanical drive)
   - Speed reading accuracy depends on tire size

7. **Tire Size Compensation**:
   - If larger tires installed: Actual speed higher than indicated
   - If smaller tires installed: Actual speed lower than indicated
   - Document tire size change for reference
   - Consider speedometer error in speed calculations

8. **Verification After Tire Change**:
   - Test speedometer against GPS speed
   - Compare indicated speed vs. actual speed
   - Document any variance
   - Note tire size in vehicle records
   - Monitor for speedometer cable wear

**Important Note**: The Unimog 435 uses a mechanical speedometer drive (not electronic calibration). After changing tire sizes, the speedometer reading will shift relative to actual vehicle speed due to the changed tire circumference. This is normal and cannot be "recalibrated" without changing the transmission gearing, which is not practical. Simply note the tire size change and adjust your speed reference accordingly.

**Barry's Answer:**

Normal hydraulic system operating pressures are critical for proper system function:

1. **Air Compressor Pressure** (Section 09/13/14):
   - Operating pressure ranges: 18, 50, 3 bar
   - Maximum operating speed: 3000 1/min
   - Emergency speed (short duration): 3500-3300 1/min
   - System monitors multiple pressure points

2. **Main Hydraulic System**:
   - Operates at pressure sufficient to power all systems
   - Steering system: Requires specific pressure for servo function
   - Winch system: High pressure for load holding
   - Raising/lowering cab: Moderate pressure sufficient

3. **Relief Valve Settings**:
   - Primary relief valve: Maximum system pressure
   - Pilot pressure: Proportional to load
   - Load-sensing valve: Adjusts flow to demand
   - Emergency relief: Prevents overpressure

4. **Component Pressure Requirements**:
   - Torque converter: 100 bar reference pressure
   - Steering hydraulics: Pressure for servo torque
   - Brake system: Backup pressure available
   - System test pressure: Measured at test connections

5. **Pressure Test Connections**:
   - Located throughout system (page 834)

6. **Temperature Effects**:

   - Hot oil: Lower pressure (thinner fluid)

7. **System Pressures by Function**:
   - Pump inlet: Atmospheric (suction)
   - Main line pressure: Varies with load
   - Pilot pressure: Proportional control pressure

   - Pressure gauges at test points
   - Regular pressure readings establish baseline
   - Deviation indicates system problems
