## Question 1: How do you adjust the parking brake on a Unimog 435?

**Section**: Parking Brake (Part 24, pages 723-758)
**Pages Found**: 733, 747-748

**Barry's Answer:**

The parking brake on your 435 is a spring-applied mechanical system. Here's how to adjust the clearance in the rear axle fixed caliper:

1. Release the parking brake completely - the actuating lever must rest on the stop
2. Remove the guard plate (valid for brake lining wear indicator)
3. Loosen the counternut on the cover side
4. Adjust the clearance to specification - refer to manual section 1.1/1 for exact clearance values
5. Direction of rotation shown in section 1.5/1
6. Tighten the counternut securely
7. For the flange side, unscrew the screw plug, adjust clearance, then screw plug back in
8. Tightening torque for screw plug: see section 1.3/1
9. Test the brakes by actuating service brake and parking brake several times
10. Check clearance multiple times to verify adjustment
11. Install the cover plate

Tools Required:
- Wrench set for counternut and screw plug
- Clearance measuring tool (reference specifications in 1.1/1)

Safety: Always ensure parking brake is fully released before adjusting. Test adjustment thoroughly before returning vehicle to service.

Cylinder head bolt torque specifications are critical - improper torque will cause gasket failure or head warping. Here are the exact specifications:

1. Torque specifications for cylinder head nuts:
   - 1st tightening stage: 60 Nm
   - 2nd tightening stage: 90 Nm
   - 3rd tightening stage: 110 Nm
   - Note: Tighten to 120 Nm at 3rd stage for first-time assembly
   - Always use tightening diagram shown in section 4.1/6

2. Torque specifications for cylinder head bolts (later engine numbers):
   - From Engine End No. 678 155:
   - 1st stage: 60 Nm
   - 2nd stage: 90 Nm
   - 3rd stage: 110 Nm
   - First assembly: 120 Nm at 3rd stage
   - Refer to tightening diagram in section 4.1/6

3. Installation procedure:
   - Oil cylinder head bolts at thread and head contact surface
   - Do NOT pour oil into threaded holes
   - Only use bolts from spare parts stock
   - Install push rods before rocker arm assembly
   - Follow tightening sequence diagram precisely

Tools Required:
- Torque wrench calibrated to 120 Nm
- Oil (multipurpose lubricant)
- Tightening diagram tool/reference
- Socket set for bolt installation

Safety: CRITICAL - improper torque causes catastrophic engine failure. Always use calibrated torque wrench. Follow sequence diagram exactly. Never reuse bolts from previous gasket failure.

Important: Refer to specific section 4.1/6 for complete tightening diagram with bolt sequence. Two different bolt types exist depending on engine number - verify your engine number before replacement.

## Question 5: How do you drain and refill the cooling system?

**Section**: Cooling System (Part 6, pages 145-173)
**Pages Found**: 149, 166, 170-172

**Barry's Answer:**

The cooling system on your 435 holds approximately 20 liters of approved coolant. Complete drain and refill is essential maintenance.

1. Drain procedure:
   - Raise cab fully - refer to section 60.5-2.1/1 or 60.5-2.2/1
   - Disconnect battery (negative terminal first, then positive)
   - Open shutoff valve at heater circuit
   - Drain coolant at engine drain plug
   - Also drain at radiator - unscrew drain plug at radiator bottom
   - Allow complete drainage

2. Cooling system capacity:
   - Total system: 20 liters (with heat exchanger)
   - Frost protection: Antifreeze to -25°C requires 8 liters
   - Frost protection: Antifreeze to -40°C requires 10.2 liters
   - Treating agent (corrosion inhibitor): 0.2 liters
   - Expansion tank: Should be 2/3 full after filling

3. Refill procedure:
   - Install drain plugs and tighten
   - Lower cab back to ground position
   - Pour approved coolant into expansion tank
   - Fill to 2/3 level initially
   - Start engine and allow to reach operating temperature
   - Add more coolant as needed to reach proper level
   - Install expansion tank cap

4. System testing:
   - Check for leaks at all connection points
   - Verify thermostat operation (refer to 5.2/1)
   - Perform leakage test at 1 bar pressure (refer to 5.1/1)
   - Check expansion tank overpressure valve opens at 0.7 bar

Specifications:
- Coolant type: Approved antifreeze (brand not specified in manual)
- Operating temperature: 95°C normal
- Thermostat start opening: 83°C
- Main valve full open: 95°C
- System pressure test: 1.0 bar

Tools Required:
- Wrench set for drain plugs
- Coolant container (minimum 25 liter capacity)
- Pressure test pump (Special tool No. 001 589 48 21 00 B)
- Cap for pressure checking (Special tool No. 605 589 00 25 00 B)

Safety: NEVER remove radiator cap while engine is hot - risk of severe coolant burns. Allow engine to cool completely before opening system. Ensure battery is disconnected before starting work. Approved antifreeze is MANDATORY - do not use plain water.

Related Systems: Radiator (page 170), coolant pump (page 166), thermostat body, heater system

Important: Antifreeze must be disposed of properly - it's toxic. Always use approved coolant type - mixing brands may cause chemical reaction. Check coolant level monthly, especially before winter season.

4. Assembly torque specifications:

## Question 7: How do you troubleshoot and repair fuel system leaks?

**Section**: Fuel System (Part 7, pages 163-200)
**Pages Found**: 165

**Barry's Answer:**

Fuel system leaks are CRITICAL safety hazards. Any fuel smell must be investigated immediately.

1. Leak identification:
   - Visually inspect all fuel lines and connections
   - Look for visible fuel drips under vehicle
   - Check fuel filter area for seepage
   - Inspect fuel pump connections
   - Examine injector lines at pump and injectors

2. Common leak locations:
   - Fuel line connections (most common)
   - Filter housing seams
   - Fuel pump mounting surface
   - Injection line banjo bolts
   - Quick-disconnect fuel couplers

3. Inspection procedure:
   - Stop engine and allow system to depressurize
   - Disconnect battery to prevent accidental fuel pump operation
   - Dry affected area with compressed air
   - Operate fuel pump briefly (reconnect battery, turn ignition on)
   - Observe if fuel leaks reappear
   - Do NOT operate engine if active leak present

4. Repair options:
   - Tighten fuel line fittings (but do NOT over-tighten - risk of crushing fuel line)
   - Replace fuel line if cracked or damaged
   - Replace fuel filter if seeping from housing
   - Replace fuel pump gasket if leaking at mounting surface
   - Replace injector line if cracked
   - Use new copper washers at all banjo bolt connections

5. System pressure test:
   - Fuel system pressure: Refer to manual fuel pump specifications
   - After repair, pressurize system and recheck for leaks
   - No leaks should appear within 5 minutes of pressurization

Tools Required:
- Fuel line wrench set (prevents line crushing)
- Fuel filter removal tool
- Container for fuel spillage
- Compressed air system
- New fuel line sections (appropriate diameter and type)
- Replacement gaskets and copper washers
- Replacement fuel filter element

Safety: FUEL IS EXTREMELY FLAMMABLE - do not smoke, use open flames, or create sparks near fuel system. Ensure proper ventilation. Always disconnect battery before working on fuel system. Have fire extinguisher (type ABC) readily available. Do NOT allow fuel to contact skin - wear nitrile gloves. If fuel system repair is beyond your capability, consult professional mechanic immediately.

Related Systems: Fuel pump (engine-mounted), fuel injectors, fuel filter, injection pump

Important: Never operate engine with known fuel leak. Replace all fuel lines every 5 years regardless of condition - fuel line degrades internally. Always use approved fuel lines with correct pressure rating (minimum 3 bar for diesel systems). Verify fuel type is correct: This engine requires diesel fuel ONLY - never use kerosene or gasoline.

## Question 9: How is the exhaust system removed and reinstalled?

**Section**: Exhaust System (Part 8, pages 201-214)
**Pages Found**: 201-210

**Barry's Answer:**

The exhaust system on your 435 connects the engine outlet to the muffler and tailpipe. Here's the removal and reinstallation procedure:

1. Pre-removal inspection:
   - Allow engine to cool completely - exhaust is EXTREMELY hot
   - Raise vehicle on proper stands if necessary
   - Inspect entire exhaust system for rust or damage
   - Locate all mounting bolts and brackets

2. Removal procedure:
   - Disconnect battery before starting
   - Remove engine hood or access covers as needed
   - Locate manifold mounting bolts (typically 4-6 bolts at engine)
   - Locate intermediate pipe connections
   - Remove muffler mounting brackets
   - Carefully separate exhaust manifold from engine head
   - Remove gasket and inspect for damage
   - Slide intermediate pipes and muffler assembly rearward
   - Note routing of system to ensure correct reinstallation

3. Inspection and cleaning:
   - Check all internal surfaces for rust or carbon buildup
   - Clean mounting surfaces on engine head with scraper
   - Inspect gasket condition - replace if damaged
   - Check all bolts for corrosion - replace if corroded
   - Verify muffler internals are intact

4. Installation procedure:
   - Install new gasket on engine head manifold outlet
   - Position exhaust manifold with mounting bolts
   - Hand-start all bolts before final tightening
   - Tighten manifold bolts in cross pattern to specification (see 1.4/1)
   - Route intermediate pipes in original path
   - Install muffler assembly, ensuring proper clearance from suspension
   - Secure all mounting brackets with bolts
   - Torque all fasteners per specification

5. Final checks:
   - Start engine and listen for unusual noises
   - Check for exhaust leaks at all connections
   - Verify no contact with fuel lines or electrical harness
   - Check proper operation of oxygen sensor if equipped

Tools Required:
- Socket wrench set (manifold bolts typically M8 or M10)
- Torque wrench for accurate fastening
- Scraper for gasket removal
- Gasket sealer (if manual specifies)
- New gasket (manifold to head)
- New exhaust clamps if original are corroded

Safety: EXHAUST IS EXTREMELY HOT - allow minimum 30 minutes cooldown after engine stop. Never work on exhaust immediately after operation. Ensure proper ventilation if working in enclosed area - carbon monoxide is deadly. Always use proper jack stands - never rely on hydraulic jack alone.

Related Systems: Engine block outlet, turbocharger outlet (if equipped), emission system

Important: Replace exhaust gasket every time manifold is removed - do not reuse old gasket. If rust perforation is visible in muffler, entire muffler assembly should be replaced. Check rubber isolation mounts - replace if cracked or deteriorated.

## Question 10: What are the procedures for repairing electrical switches and relays on the dashboard?

**Section**: Electrical Wiring (Part 14, pages 403-440)
**Pages Found**: 403-412

**Barry's Answer:**

Dashboard switches and relays control lighting, wipers, heating, and other systems. Repair procedures vary by component type.

1. Pre-repair safety:
   - Disconnect battery (negative terminal first)
   - Allow minimum 10 minutes for system discharge
   - Use electrostatic discharge (ESD) protection if working with modern electronics
   - Never work on live circuits

2. Switch replacement procedure:
   - Locate problem switch on dashboard
   - Trace wiring harness to connector
   - Document wire positions (take photo if possible)
   - Disconnect connector carefully
   - Remove switch retaining screws or clips
   - Install new switch in reverse order
   - Verify all wires are properly seated in connector
   - Reconnect battery and test operation

3. Relay service:
   - Locate relay in fuse/relay box (typically under cab or in engine compartment)
   - Note relay position (relays are not interchangeable)
   - Safely remove relay by pulling straight out
   - Inspect relay for corrosion or damage
   - If relay contact points are corroded, clean carefully with fine emery cloth
   - Reinstall relay, ensuring proper seating
   - If relay does not respond, replace with identical part number

4. Common issues and solutions:
   - Switch sticks: Clean contact points or replace switch
   - Relay chatter: Replace relay immediately
   - Dim lights: Check relay contact resistance with multimeter
   - Intermittent operation: Check connector for corrosion, clean if necessary
   - Burned contacts: Replace component, do not attempt repair

5. Testing after repair:
   - Reconnect battery
   - Test affected circuit multiple times
   - Verify proper operation before reinstalling panels
   - Check for voltage at relay coil (typically 12V)
   - Verify load circuit operates correctly

Tools Required:
- Socket wrench set (screws are typically 4-6mm)
- Connector removal tool set
- Fine emery cloth for contact cleaning
- Multimeter for electrical testing
- Wire crimpers (if replacing terminals)
- Replacement switches and relays (exact part numbers critical)
- Dielectric grease for connector protection

Safety: CRITICAL - always disconnect battery before electrical work. Never work on live circuits. Use proper insulated tools. Do not touch circuit board traces. If you are not comfortable with electrical work, consult professional technician.

Related Systems: Main electrical harness, fuse box, lighting circuits, wiper motor circuits, heating circuits

Important: Always use OEM (original equipment manufacturer) parts for switches and relays. Non-OEM relays may have different contact ratings and cause circuit damage. Keep detailed records of which switches were replaced and when. If multiple switches fail simultaneously, suspect wiring problem, not switch failure. Consider having complete electrical system checked by professional if widespread failures occur.
